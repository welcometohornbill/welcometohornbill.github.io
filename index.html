What to look for

Reading Naga attire

Traditional dress is a language. Colours, patterns and ornaments can show a person's tribe, clan and standing.

🧣

Shawls

The most important garment. Each tribe has its own patterns, and some shawls could only be worn by those who earned them through bravery or by hosting feasts of merit. The Ao warrior shawl, with its painted white band, is a famous example.

🪶

Headgear

Warrior headdresses often carry hornbill feathers, a symbol of valour. The bird is now protected, so many performers use replicas.

📿

Ornaments

Boar tusks, cowrie and conch shells, brass, and treasured necklaces of coloured beads.

👗

Women's dress

Woven wraparound skirts whose colours and stripes vary by tribe, worn with bead necklaces and shawls.

🛡️

Weapons as dress

Spears, daos and shields complete the ceremonial dress for war dances.

✒️

Tattoos

Among the Konyak, facial and body tattoos once marked warriors and life events. A few elders still carry them.

At Kisama, ask at each morung what their dress means. Most people are happy to explain.
Then and now

A short history

Key moments behind the land and the festival.

Ancient times

The morung

A dormitory for young men (and in some tribes young women) that served as a school for skills, discipline, history and village defence. The houses at Kisama are modelled on them.

Always

The hornbill

The Great Indian Hornbill runs through Naga folklore, songs and dress as a symbol of courage, beauty and harmony. It gives the festival its name.

1944

The Battle of Kohima

From April to June, one of the fiercest battles of the Second World War was fought here, halting the Japanese advance into India. The tennis-court battlefield is now part of the Kohima War Cemetery.

1 Dec 1963

Statehood

Nagaland became the 16th state of India. That is why the festival opens on 1 December, Statehood Day.

2000

Hornbill Festival begins

The Government of Nagaland launched the festival to bring all tribes together, build inter-tribal understanding and share Naga heritage with the world.

Today

The Festival of Festivals

Ten days gather many separate tribal celebrations into one. English is the official language, and Nagamese is widely spoken between tribes.

Be a good guest

Local etiquette

Nagas are known for warm hospitality. A few simple courtesies go a long way.

Please do

  • Ask before photographing people, especially elders and performers in traditional dress.
  • Accept tea or food when offered, even a small taste. It is a gesture of welcome.
  • Dress warmly and modestly when visiting villages and churches.
  • Buy crafts directly from artisans. It supports village families.
  • Greet with a smile and a handshake. A simple "thank you" is always appreciated.
  • Keep your Inner Line Permit (ILP) or official ID handy while travelling.

Please don't

  • Touch ceremonial weapons, drums, headgear or costumes without permission.
  • Litter. Nagaland's villages take great pride in keeping clean.
  • Enter private homes, granaries or morungs unless invited.
  • Make assumptions or jokes about tribal customs or food.
  • Travel on Sunday expecting shops to be open. Many close for church.
  • Underestimate the king chilli. Taste it very carefully.
